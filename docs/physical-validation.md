# Physical validation and tuning

Rev B has analytical geometry, manufacturer references and software tests. No airflow, acoustic, thermal, force, endurance or physical assembly result is claimed here. This plan defines the measurements needed to turn the development design into a tested prototype. Record a pass, failure or explicit outstanding dependency for every test; do not label an unbuilt mechanism or unexecuted test as passed.

The current status is in [deliverable-status.md](deliverable-status.md). Build dependencies include the completed carrier PCB, component restraints, guards/interlock/linkage cover, source-matched collision and insertion checks, and Onshape integration. These are design work, separate from uncertainties that require hardware. Use [printing and assembly](printing-and-assembly.md), [calibration](calibration.md), [firmware operation](firmware-operation.md), [wiring](../hardware/wiring.md) and the [power budget](../hardware/power-budget.md) as the controlled references.

## Airflow design and what must be demonstrated

The axial head replaces two tilted fans and a turning plenum with one Noctua NF-A12x25 G2 PWM and a continuous inlet-to-outlet passage. Removing a bend and stream merge offers a plausible reduction in losses and turbulent mixing, but one fan has a different operating curve from two fans. The original version remains the comparison baseline if a prototype exists. Free-air volume, shutoff pressure and sound ratings do not occur simultaneously and do not predict the assembled product's performance.

| Starting geometry | Meaning and limitation |
|---|---|
| 114 mm throat, 14 mm bell-mouth radius | Throat area about 10,207 mm²; radius/throat-diameter ratio 0.123. The 142 mm mouth joins the throat with a true quarter-circle profile. It should reduce sharp-edge separation, but the printed finish, rear guard and nearby desk/wall can alter the benefit. |
| Circle-to-rounded-rectangle duct | A gradual transition to the 104 × 94 mm nominal outlet. Rounded corners and any local fillets reduce actual area slightly from the rectangular gross calculation. Check the generated sections rather than using only outer dimensions. |
| Seven radial vanes, 0.8 mm thick, 18 mm long | A compact trial straightener after an initial 15 mm separation from the fan's downstream envelope. Flat radial vanes can oppose tangential velocity, but their incidence, wake and blockage can also add loss and tones. This is not a measured swirl-matched stator. |
| Two 55 mm panels with one common yoke | Equal and opposite rotation forms a gradual height contraction. Linkages stay outside the main air path. Unlike planar shutter blades, the panels turn the wall inward along an axial length. |
| 104 × 94 mm open gross area | 9,776 mm² before grille blockage and boundary layers. The design seeks high normal-mode volume with the panels open. |
| Initial 75% minimum gross area | 7,332 mm², 70.5 mm exit height and 12.3355° inward panel angle. It is a starting test limit, not a demonstrated optimum. The firmware forbids reducing this ratio below 0.75. |

For width `W`, open height `H`, panel length `L` and inward angle `theta`, gross outlet area is `A = W × (H − 2 L sin(theta))`. In incompressible flow, mean axial velocity at a section is `Q/A`. If boost reduces area to 0.75 of open area, mean velocity improves only if the new volume flow remains above 0.75 of open volume. A provisional 10% velocity improvement requires `Qboost/Qopen > 0.825` at that area ratio. Real downstream jet measurements must also account for the fixed grille, spreading and velocity profile. A smaller opening alone does not establish useful boost.

The selected fan regulates speed. Restriction can substantially change flow while tach RPM remains near its commanded value. Use tach for motor diagnostics and an independent differential-pressure measurement for restriction supervision; neither replaces velocity/volume measurements. The existing wall tap measures a local static-pressure difference relative to ambient. It does not directly measure total pressure, fan efficiency or volume flow, and it can be biased by tap placement, swirl or blocked tubing.

## Measurement record and equipment

For each run, record CAD/source revision, firmware/UF2 hash, board revision, calibration record, fan/servo/LED board identity, print material/profile, configuration, ambient temperature, supply/cable, setup dimensions, instrument range/calibration and measurement uncertainty. Photograph the setup and keep raw samples. Repeat at least three settled readings per operating point, including an increasing and decreasing control sweep.

Use a differential manometer/reference within the sensor's useful low-pressure range, a suitably sized calibrated axial velocity probe or characterized low-pressure flow hood, an independent tachometer, a meter, an oscilloscope, a current measurement fixture/electronic load, contact thermocouples or another reference thermometer, a force gauge, angle gauge and calipers. For noise comparison use a calibrated sound-level instrument where possible; a phone recording is useful for identifying tones and rattles but is not an absolute sound rating. Keep temporary probes secured outside moving parts. Work with guarded hardware and an accessible electrical disconnect.

Log time series fast enough for the phenomenon: ordinary flow/thermal trends can use at least 10 samples/s and 1 sample/s respectively, while supply/PWM and short shutdown events need oscilloscope traces. Log controller state and commanded/measured position alongside measurements so a pressure-limited run is not mistaken for the requested full boost point.

## Bench sequence and aerodynamic comparison

1. Complete dry assembly and basic electrical commissioning. Measure both temperature channels, voltage scales, pressure zero/polarity, independent tach agreement and all five panel calibration points using the exact commands in [calibration.md](calibration.md). Do not disable guards or protections to make a test reach an intended setting.
2. Fix the fan head horizontal at its intended desk height. Leave a documented clear space around the inlet, initially at least one inlet diameter behind it, and a still-air measurement zone in front. Record nearby walls and desk reflections. Later repeat at realistic restricted inlet spacing; the clearance recommendation must come from these results.
3. With the full guarded enclosure and panels open, measure at encoder settings 10%, 25%, 50% and 75%. Confirm minimum duty starts reliably from rest, not only when approaching it from high speed. After each setting change allow at least 10 seconds to settle; average stable data for a further 10 seconds. Extend the interval if pressure, RPM or velocity is still changing.
4. Sweep 75%, 80%, 85%, 90%, 95% and 100% encoder settings upward and back downward. The fan should remain at maximum commissioned PWM while actual closure follows the requested area. Record both panel angles, pressure mean/variation, RPM, input and servo current, velocity/volume, sound and any visible movement. Stop and correct rubbing, chatter or a pressure/fault event; do not increase thresholds to conceal it.
5. Repeat open and selected boost points with and without the trial straightener, maintaining identical fixed-guard support and all other geometry. Use a non-vane locating ring if needed for capture. Test a honeycomb only if measured residual swirl justifies a separate low-loss trial; it is not part of the default design.
6. Select the maximum useful closure from the measured tradeoff. Prefer a less-constricted endpoint when volume loss, pressure fluctuation, noise or jet nonuniformity increases without a repeatable throw benefit. Reduce closure through a larger `minAreaRatio`, then repeat the complete affected calibration evidence. Keep the normal/boost threshold at 75% initially; tune it within 60–90% only after finding a useful boost range.

Measure a traverse plane beyond the grille with a grid covering the whole jet, including its low-velocity edges. Start with at least a 7 × 7 equal-area grid; refine around strong gradients and repeat with a finer grid until integrated flow changes less than the selected uncertainty allowance. Use the axial velocity component, not an omnidirectional speed magnitude in swirling flow. Estimate `Q = sum(v_axial,i × cell_area_i)` and report grid extent and probe blockage. A centreline reading multiplied by gross outlet area is not volume flow. A flow hood or collection duct changes system pressure unless characterized; compare its readings with/without the fixture and report the correction rather than assuming it is nonintrusive.

For useful throw, map the centreline and cross-jet profile at 0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 and 2.0 m, extending as needed. Use **1.0 m/s axial velocity** as an initial engineering threshold for reporting throw distance, and report jet width above that threshold at the same stations. This is a comparison metric, not a promised comfort specification. Repeat at the user's likely desk distance. A very fast centreline with a tiny useful area should not be described as improved overall airflow.

To assess swirl, use an instrument that resolves flow direction or a secured noncontact visualization method outside the guards. Compare tangential/axial flow or measured flow angle at matching radial points. Do not insert loose threads near the rotor. Retain the straightener only if the reduction in swirl or profile variation exceeds measurement uncertainty and its pressure/noise cost is acceptable. A provisional target is no more than 5% loss of normal volume flow and no more than 1 dBA added noise for a repeatable improvement in directionality. These are prototype decision criteria, not existing results.

The bell-mouth comparison should keep throat, fan, guards and downstream configuration constant. Use a sharp-edge or smaller-radius inlet trial only as a controlled sample, with guards fitted and no exposed rotor. Compare volume, inlet noise and near-lip flow behavior. Test radius changes within the available envelope and inspect whether the rear guard obstructs the rounded acceleration region. Do not run CFD on the modified impeller in Noctua's public integration CAD and present it as an accurate fan simulation.

## Provisional engineering acceptance matrix

These criteria are internal prototype targets. They are not product safety certification or manufacturer limits. Where an exact manufacturer limit applies, use the stricter supported limit and record it.

| Item | Measurement / initial acceptance |
|---|---|
| Normal airflow | Stable starts at minimum PWM across at least 20 cold/warm attempts; no contact or hunting. Report delivered volume at each normal point, with the assembled guards and grille. |
| Useful boost | At least 10% repeatable mean/centre-region axial velocity or throw improvement over 75% control, exceeding instrument uncertainty, while retaining at least 80% of open volume. Both measured values must be reported; the 10% mean-velocity target at 75% area may require more than 80% volume retention. If no endpoint meets a worthwhile tradeoff, reduce constriction and record the result. |
| Pressure margin | At the chosen endpoint, steady local pressure should remain below the 18 Pa soft limit by at least the larger of 2 Pa or measured uncertainty plus observed variation. No unexplained oscillation or repeated limiter cycling. Keep 24 Pa hard protection active. |
| Nozzle tracking | Both panels agree within 0.5° initially and return repeatably; no contact or clamp loosening over 100 slow manual/powered sweeps. Firmware feedback remains comfortably inside its trip tolerance, not just below it. |
| Servo capacity | Measure the worst manual yoke force with the fan off, then add analytical pressure load and gravity/acceleration allowances. Use the actual linkage mechanical advantage at each point. Target at least 3× margin to the supplier's 4.8 V stall torque as an initial screening check; stall torque is not a continuous rating. Also require acceptable current, temperature, noise and tracking in the endurance test. |
| Noise | Measure at 1 m on the outlet axis and at the intended user's position with fixed room geometry. Aim initially for normal full-power sound no greater than 35 dBA and boost increase no greater than 3 dBA, with no new tonal whistle, buzz or rattle. These targets may require revision based on achievable airflow. Background should be at least 10 dB below the measured fan level for a simple uncorrected comparison; otherwise report the limitation. |
| Grille retention | Test the actual four-pair gap and final skins/keepers, not catalog zero-gap magnet pull. Initial target: at least 5 N distributed axial retention, no spontaneous release/rattle during maximum operation, and intentional edge-peel removal below 15 N. A 24 Pa pressure over the gross open area is only about 0.235 N; handling, warping and keeper strength dominate. |
| Guard/cover rigidity | Use suitable guarded-access gauges and controlled static deflection tests on the fixed front/rear guards and linkage cover. Require no contact with rotor/linkage or detached fragment under the chosen handling load. Nominal 5–6 mm slots do not alone establish safe reach distance or impact resistance. Define applicable product-access qualification before a consumer release. |
| TPU isolation | Compare rigid and TPU mounting at matched settings with desk vibration/accelerometer and acoustic measurements. Require no contact/rattle or alignment loss. Measure pad thickness and fan alignment initially, after 24 hours assembled and after a hot run; permanent set must not consume rotor/duct clearance. |
| Encoder/base | Upward rim movement increases output, all detents count correctly, and axial pressing does not drag the wheel or bend its board. The base must not slide or tip during the measured approximately 6 N encoder press on the intended desk surfaces. Repeat after foot wear and cable loading. |
| LEDs/diffusers | Every pixel/color and the separate status bar work, no electrical contact with retainers, no direct glare at the seated user's sightline. Confirm the normal/boost boundary is clear and night mode leaves only required fault indication. |

## Power and temperature qualification

Verify a negotiated 15 V / at least 2 A PD contract before enabling loads. Measure fan voltage at its connector, logic at the actual load and servo voltage during motion. Fan voltage must stay below the selected fan's 13.2 V operating maximum; target its nominal 12 V. The firmware qualification ranges are 14–16 V bus and 4.65–5.35 V logic. Check supply current and regulator temperatures against the actual component limits and the [18.05 W analytical peak budget](../hardware/power-budget.md), including all 24 pixels, the servo allowance and conversion losses.

Use an electronic load for the defined peak rail test rather than deliberately stalling a servo for a prolonged period. Test PD connection, startup ramp, maximum fan, fastest allowed servo motion, configured lighting and a bounded full-white LED test. The last exceeds normal cosmetic settings but verifies the budget. Observe current limiting, rail rise/fall, no MCU brownout and no unexpected PD renegotiation. A limiting eFuse can produce a sag before shutdown; record the waveform, not only a meter average.

Run the completed enclosure at its highest sustained commissioned operating point for at least two hours, and until every monitored hot spot changes less than 1°C over 15 minutes. Repeat at a documented warm ambient representative of intended use. Measure regulator packages/boards, load switches, eFuse region, fan motor area, connectors and adjacent printed surfaces with independent probes as well as both MCP9808 channels. TEMP1 must track the regulator hot region, while TEMP2 monitors fan/base air. Establish the offset between each sensor and the actual hot spot so 55°C warning and 65°C trip occur with useful margin. Move/couple a sensor if it measures unrelated air instead of the protected component.

Repeat a reduced-load run with the main fan stopped to establish that base ventilation does not depend on rotor flow. Any regulator thermal shutdown, softened support, connector discoloration, unstable rail, or temperature continuing to rise without margin is a failure. Correct the heat path, load or enclosure before raising software thresholds. The current design has no battery and no charging tests; the small light bar reports power/service/fault status.

## Firmware and fault tests on the physical board

Host tests validate simulated control behavior; this matrix validates real IO, timing and load consequences. Prefer electrically simulated faults with rated fixtures to hazardous mechanical obstruction. In each test record the stimulus time, detected state, load-enable outputs, rail voltages, rotor coast, servo behavior, status indication and acknowledgment result. Essential fault indication must remain visible in night mode.

| Test | Expected observation |
|---|---|
| Normal commissioned startup | Power qualifies; nozzle reaches verified open before fan operation. Main and ambient lights activate; fan/main bar perform the 1.8 s smooth ramp and 0.9 s return. Saved boost is applied only after return. Confirm scope duty and mechanical motion, not animation alone. |
| Saved off, zero and night restart | Saved off/zero causes no full-speed pulse. Night suppresses the unnecessary ramp animation and uses configured dim/off lighting while normal fan control remains available. |
| User input during startup | Rotation remains responsive; short off press cancels the ramp. A 1.2 s hold toggles night once and does not also toggle power on release. |
| Normal/boost sweep | At the threshold, fan reaches commissioned maximum. Above it, PWM remains at that maximum and closure increases monotonically. Main color changes to boost and setting display continues across the full range. |
| Tach loss/abnormal RPM | With a guarded running fan, interrupt the tach signal using an electrical test connector. Confirm the documented startup grace and subsequent low-RPM fault, fan/servo disable and no repeated automatic restart. Use a pulse simulator for excess-RPM cases. |
| Pressure rise | Use a low-pressure calibrated fixture at the sensor within its range, or a secured controlled restriction. At the soft threshold closure reduces; at the hard threshold it opens and duty is capped, then persistent excess latches a fault. Never close the airflow path fully. |
| Pressure signal failure | Disconnect the sensor, simulate invalid data or reverse a small known differential. Confirm nozzle opening/duty limiting. Separately block/disconnect tubing: a plausible near-zero value can escape electronic diagnosis, so record this limitation and establish inspection/zero checks. |
| Servo feedback/PG failure | Disconnect or simulate feedback with an appropriately rated test fixture; verify tracking timeout disconnects the fan and servo. An unpowered FS90-FB does not spring open. Verify motor supply removal without assuming mechanical recovery. |
| Servo mechanical overload | First prove electrical feedback-fault response without a jam. Any later fixture overload test needs independent current/time limiting within documented servo limits. If the supplier provides no allowable stalled duration, defer physical jam testing rather than repeatedly holding the servo against a stop. |
| Thermal warning/trip | Use controlled sensor heating or a separately identified sensor-input test fixture/build. Verify warning opens panels, limits fan and removes cosmetic load; cool below hysteresis to clear warning. Severe heat or sensor loss must latch a fault. Restore the production firmware and repeat a normal startup afterward. |
| Power/PG fault | Use a suitable low-voltage bench/PD test fixture to introduce sag, inadequate contract or PG failure. Confirm supply supervision removes loads and no unsafe restart follows. Never modify the mains adapter. |
| Grille removal | With fixed guards fitted, release the removable grille from outside the protected area. Confirm switch opening disconnects commanded loads; measure rotor coast separately. Refit and acknowledge: fan must remain off until deliberately started. |
| Watchdog / stuck loop | In a labeled diagnostic build or controlled debugger test, stop loop progress. Verify reset followed by a latched watchdog fault and no automatic fan start. Restore the production UF2 before release. |
| Service entry and timeout | Jumper plus boot hold enters service. Ordinary long press, absent jumper or brief press does not. Servo jogs and fan tests time out; `fan 0` removes both test powers. Commissioning requires all five stable captures, pressure zero and min/max RPM evidence. |
| Fault acknowledgment | With the fault cause cleared, short press/`ack` returns to an off state. With required health conditions absent, acknowledgment cannot restore operation. Night settings do not mask red fault indication. |

For persistent settings, save after at least 8 seconds of inactivity and 60 seconds since the prior attempt, then power-cycle and compare every setting. Test at least 20 normal power cycles across normal/boost/night/off combinations. With a controlled power-cut fixture, interrupt power before, during and after a settings write for at least 50 varied cuts. Accept the new valid record or a prior valid record; reject a corrupted record, unintended commissioning, uncontrolled startup or an automatic filesystem erase. Confirm recovery after a deliberately invalid record on a spare/test filesystem. Record the test build and restore production settings afterward.

After initial qualification, run at least 1,000 full allowed nozzle cycles under guarded operating conditions and a 24-hour continuous fan run at a representative high load. These are screening tests, not a lifetime claim. Inspect keys, pivot sleeves, yoke slots, collars, heat-set inserts, magnets, fan alignment and servo current periodically and at the end. Re-measure backlash, noise, friction and TPU set. Increase test duration based on observed wear rather than interpreting a clean first run as lifetime reliability.

## Parameters and decisions to freeze

| Parameter group | Tune using / freeze when |
|---|---|
| Bell-mouth radius, throat and inlet clearance | Controlled inlet comparison, available envelope, guard clearance and delivered-flow/noise results. |
| Transition sections and fixed outlet width/height | Printed surface/area checks and velocity profile; changes must update firmware geometry constants and calibration. |
| Vane count, thickness, axial length and spacing | With/without insert loss, directionality and tone measurements. Keep FDM wall thickness achievable. |
| Panel length, minimum area and maximum angle | Measured flow/throw/pressure tradeoff, matched panel travel and stop margin. Never commission travel into the mechanical stop. |
| Crank/rod lengths, key and guide fits | Actual servo/horn dimensions, insertion/tool access, friction, backlash and cycling. Recompute kinematics after changing them. |
| PWM minimum/maximum, RPM baseline and boost threshold | Reliable start tests, motor data and the useful normal/boost range. Tach baseline belongs to this fan and enclosure. |
| Five servo pulse/feedback points | Physical angle gauges with the final hardware, supply, divider and shaft indexing. |
| Pressure zero/thresholds and tubing/tap location | Reference manometer, startup and live fluctuation data, blocked-tube inspection plan and chosen nozzle endpoint. |
| Temperature locations/thresholds | Measured sensor-to-hot-spot relation, hot enclosed tests and material/electrical margins. |
| LED counts, normal/night brightness and diffuser thickness | Actual boards, viewing conditions, power/heat and individual LED tests. |
| Encoder direction, shaft clearance and shroud | Actual detents/press travel, horizontal user interaction and base stability. |
| Magnet pocket/skin gap and retention | Actual paired pull/peel, warped-print tolerance, keeper integrity and repeated cleaning. |
| TPU geometry, compression and desk feet | Alignment, vibration/noise comparison, creep and user press stability. |
| Inserts, shafts, fasteners and harness lengths | Final CAD assembly, coupon fits, physical access and service withdrawal. |

Final release evidence comprises the source/CAD revision and manifests, purchased-model provenance and actual revision checks, complete PCB files/checks, populated BOM, wiring continuity record, firmware hash, calibration record, print profiles, assembly photos, raw measurement logs and disposition of every failed test. Clearly list any test still outstanding. The remaining physical uncertainties are airflow and throw, pressure-tap behavior, noise, actual servo torque/current and fit, print tolerances/wear, grille/guard strength, magnetic holding force, TPU creep, electronics heat, USB insertion strain and real power-loss storage behavior. They require hardware; further documentation cannot establish their outcomes.
